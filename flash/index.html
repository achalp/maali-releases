Back to the garden
Bring a controller back to life

Put Maali on a fresh board, from your browser.

If a controller board ever gives out, you do not wait on us for a replacement. Buy the same board off the shelf, plug it into this computer, and this page writes Maali onto it. No software to install, no cable but the one in the box.

First, which board is it?

The two take different firmware. Pick yours and the button appears; the confirmation window names the board again before anything is written.

This browser can't talk to the board. Flashing needs Chrome or Edge on a laptop or desktop (they carry the connection this uses; Safari and Firefox do not, and phones can't do it at all). Open this page in Chrome or Edge and the button above will come to life.
Three things to get right

It must be the same board. Maali is fitted to the Waveshare ESP32-S3-RS485-CAN (external-antenna version) — the exact one on the parts list. A different ESP32 board has its wires in different places and will not work. If in doubt, order the board from the parts list.

If the page cannot connect, put the board into its programming mode by hand. Hold BOOT, press and release RESET, then release BOOT. The board now waits for the write and runs nothing else, which is what you want when whatever is on it keeps restarting ("the device has been lost" is how the page reports that). Flash, then press RESET once more at the end.

This is a clean slate. A fresh flash gives you a brand-new Maali with no yard, no network, and no password — exactly like the day you first built one. When it restarts it puts up its own setup network (look for maali-XXXXXX); join it, open http://192.168.4.1, and walk the setup again. If you can still reach the old controller, download a settings backup first (its Settings page, under The controller) — then restore it on the new board and you are done in a minute instead of rebuilding the yard by hand.

Five things to get right

Power it from the cabinet first. The Maali board's USB socket carries data only; it runs on the 24 VAC at its J7 terminal and nothing else. Have the transformer connected and on before you plug in the USB cable, and keep it on while the page writes.

Press the little RESET button when it is done. After the write the board sits waiting for more, and a cable alone cannot wake it. Press the button marked RESET (SW2), or switch the 24 VAC off and on. The status light and the setup network tell you it is up.

If the page cannot connect, put the board into its programming mode by hand. Hold BOOT (SW1), press and release RESET (SW2), then release BOOT. The board now waits for the write and runs nothing else, which is what you want when whatever is on it keeps restarting ("the device has been lost" is how the page reports that). Flash, then press RESET once more at the end.

Brand-new board? Leave the valve wires off for the first start. The small chip that guards the valves is written by Maali itself the first time it boots, which takes a few seconds. Until that has happened a new chip can hold zone 8 on, and the check that follows clicks zone 1 for a fraction of a second. A board that has run Maali before needs none of this.

How it goes

  1. Pick the board above. The firmware is cut to the board; the wrong one will not work and the page will not offer it until you choose.
  2. Plug it into this computer with a USB-C cable — the same port you would use to charge a phone. The Maali board also needs its 24 VAC on.
  3. Press the button and, when the little window asks, choose the board from the list of ports. The window names the board you picked; read it before you confirm.
  4. Wait about a minute. It erases, writes, and checks itself. Do not unplug it while it works.
  5. Fit it back in the cabinet and walk the setup, or restore your backup. The garden is watered again.

Nothing here leaves your computer except the one download of the firmware itself. The writing happens locally, over the cable, between this page and the board. Prefer the command line? The same images are published with each release as maali-factory-waveshare-8ch.bin and maali-factory-maali-v2.bin, flashable with esptool at offset 0x0.
